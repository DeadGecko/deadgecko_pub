Samhain

The last night of the harvest, when the veil runs thin and the old ones come to the fire.

Samhain falls at sundown on October 31: summer ends, winter begins, and for one night the Otherworld walks close. Every year two hosts keep the fire. Who else steps out of the dark is their call.

High proof, by the dropper, not the glass. A private tasting, ahead of licensing.

The hosts

The forest and the winter. One keeps the year’s last green; the other brings the first frost. Samhain is the one night they share the fire.

Host

Cernunnos

The antlered one of the old Gaulish world, lord of the wild beasts. On the Gundestrup cauldron he sits with a torc at his throat and another raised in his hand, a stag at his side. He keeps the forest side of the fire.

Host

Cailleach

The winter queen of the Highlands, young tonight; she ages as the season turns. Her staff brings the frost and mountains fell from her creel. In Glen Lyon her family of stones goes back into their house at Samhain. Her husband and daughter are home in the glen. She is out.

Cernunnos’s guests

Forest, bronze and smoke.

Guest

Púca

Ireland’s shapeshifter, most often a dark horse with golden eyes. Samhain is his night: after it the blackberries are his, and asked politely he may give a true answer. Trickster, not villain.

Guest

Gwyn ap Nudd

King of Annwn, the Welsh Otherworld, with a hall said to lie under Glastonbury Tor. On Calan Gaeaf, the Welsh Samhain, he rides out with the Wild Hunt and his white, red-eared hounds, gathering souls.

The Cailleach’s guests

Winter, sea and stone.

Guest

Muileartach

A one-eyed sea hag out of Lochlann in the old Fenian ballads. She sailed on Ireland, cut through the Fianna and turned down Fionn’s gold. She comes up from the storm tonight as a drowned queen.

Guest

Mongfind

An Irish queen who schemed for her sons’ crown. She poisoned her brother and drank from the cup herself so he would trust it. She died at Samhain, and the night once carried her name.
